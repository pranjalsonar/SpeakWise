Today we can find almost any piece of information in seconds. Six hundred years ago, things were very different. A single book could take months to produce, and only rich people, churches and universities could afford one. Then a new machine appeared in Germany that changed how knowledge travelled. It was the printing press, and many historians call it one of the most important inventions in human history.

## Books Before Printing

In medieval Europe, books were copied by hand, usually by monks working in monasteries. They wrote every letter with a pen, often on expensive animal skin called parchment. One mistake could mean starting a page again. A large book might take a single person a year or more. Because books were so rare and costly, very few people learned to read.

Printing itself was not completely new. In China, people printed text using carved wooden blocks more than a thousand years ago. Around the year 1040, a Chinese inventor named Bi Sheng created movable type made of clay. In Korea, a book called the Jikji was printed with metal type in 1377. These were great achievements, but the system did not spread to Europe.

## Gutenberg's Invention

Around 1440, in the German city of Mainz, a goldsmith named Johannes Gutenberg began working on a new system. His idea was to cast individual letters in metal. These letters could be arranged into words and lines, inked, pressed onto paper and then rearranged for the next page.

Gutenberg's genius was in combining several ideas. He developed a mould to make large numbers of identical metal letters quickly. He created an oil-based ink that stuck well to metal. He adapted a screw press, similar to those used to squeeze grapes for wine, to press the paper evenly. Around 1455, he completed his most famous work, the Gutenberg Bible. Copies that survive today are among the most valuable books in the world.

## A Revolution in Knowledge

The new method spread with amazing speed. Within about fifty years, there were printing shops in hundreds of European towns, and millions of books had been printed. Books became much cheaper, so more people could own them. As more books were available, more people learned to read.

Printing helped new ideas travel fast. During the Renaissance, ancient Greek and Roman texts were printed and studied widely. In 1517, Martin Luther's arguments against the Church were printed and spread across Germany in weeks, leading to the Protestant Reformation. Scientists could share their discoveries accurately, with the same diagrams in every copy. This made it easier to check and build on each other's work, which helped the scientific revolution.

Printing also helped to standardise languages. When many people read the same printed books, spelling and grammar became more consistent. Newspapers, posters and pamphlets later allowed ordinary people to follow news and take part in public debates.

## Printing Around the World

Printing presses gradually reached every part of the world. In India, a press was set up in Goa in the 1550s, and later presses printed books and newspapers in many Indian languages. These newspapers played an important role in spreading ideas about reform and, later, independence. Gutenberg himself did not become rich from his invention, as he had money problems and lost control of his workshop. Yet his idea outlived him and shaped the modern world.

## Conclusion

The printing press turned books from rare treasures into everyday objects. It spread knowledge, encouraged reading and gave new ideas a powerful voice. Many people compare it to the internet, another invention that changed how we share information. Both remind us that when knowledge becomes easier to share, societies can change in surprising and lasting ways.
