More than four hundred years after his death, William Shakespeare is still the most famous writer in the English language. His plays are performed every day somewhere in the world, from grand theatres in London to school halls in small towns. They have been translated into almost every major language and turned into hundreds of films. Why does one writer from so long ago still matter so much to us today?

## His Life

William Shakespeare was born in 1564 in Stratford-upon-Avon, a market town in England. His father made gloves and was an important man in the town. Young William probably went to the local grammar school, where he would have studied Latin and read classical stories. At eighteen, he married Anne Hathaway, and they had three children.

In his twenties, Shakespeare moved to London, where theatre was becoming very popular. He worked as an actor and a writer, and he became a part-owner of a theatre company. Many of his plays were performed at the Globe Theatre, a round, open-air building by the River Thames. Poor audience members paid a penny to stand near the stage, while richer people sat in covered seats. Shakespeare died in 1616, and seven years later his friends collected his plays in a book now called the First Folio. Without it, about half of his plays might have been lost.

## His Plays and Poems

Shakespeare wrote around thirty-seven plays, as well as 154 short poems called sonnets. His plays are usually divided into three groups. Comedies, such as A Midsummer Night's Dream and Twelfth Night, are full of mistaken identities, clever jokes and happy endings, often with marriages. Tragedies, such as Romeo and Juliet, Hamlet, Macbeth and King Lear, follow characters whose mistakes or weaknesses lead to terrible endings. Histories, such as Henry V, tell stories about English kings.

His characters feel like real people. Hamlet cannot decide whether to take revenge for his father's murder. Macbeth is a brave soldier whose ambition turns him into a murderer. Othello is a great general destroyed by jealousy. These characters struggle with problems that people still face today.

## A Gift to the English Language

Shakespeare had an enormous vocabulary, and he loved playing with words. He is often credited with being the first person to write down many words and phrases that we still use. Expressions like break the ice, wild goose chase, a heart of gold and in a pickle all appear in his plays. Even if some of them existed before, his plays made them famous.

Some of his lines are known around the world. To be, or not to be, that is the question, from Hamlet, is perhaps the most famous line in literature. All the world's a stage, from As You Like It, describes life as a play in which everyone has many roles.

## Why He Still Matters

Shakespeare's plays explore universal themes: love and friendship, power and ambition, jealousy, betrayal, family conflict and the search for identity. These feelings are the same in every age and culture, which is why his stories can be set anywhere. Bollywood films, Japanese cinema and modern teen movies have all borrowed from his plots. For example, the film West Side Story is based on Romeo and Juliet.

His language can seem difficult at first, because English has changed since his time. But when his plays are performed by good actors, the emotions become clear. Many people find that watching a play is the best way to understand him.

## Conclusion

William Shakespeare was a glove maker's son who became the most celebrated writer in English. Through his plays and poems, he showed the full range of human emotions and enriched the language we speak. His work reminds us that great stories never really grow old.
