Imagine a place in space where gravity is so strong that nothing can escape from it, not even light, the fastest thing in the universe. This is a black hole. For many years black holes were just an idea in scientists' equations. Today we know they are real, and they are some of the strangest and most fascinating objects in the universe.

## What Is a Black Hole?

Gravity is the force that pulls things together. The more mass something has, and the more tightly that mass is packed, the stronger its gravity near it. Earth's gravity keeps our feet on the ground. A black hole is what happens when a huge amount of mass is squeezed into an incredibly small space.

Around every black hole there is an invisible boundary called the event horizon. It is the point of no return. Anything that crosses it, whether gas, a planet or a beam of light, can never come back out. Because no light escapes, the black hole itself looks completely black. We can only see it through its effects on things around it.

## How Black Holes Are Born

Stars shine because of nuclear reactions in their centres. These reactions push outwards, while gravity pulls inwards. For most of a star's life, these two forces are balanced. When a very large star, many times heavier than our Sun, runs out of fuel, gravity wins. The core collapses in a fraction of a second, and the outer layers may explode in a giant blast called a supernova. If the remaining core is heavy enough, it becomes a black hole.

These are called stellar black holes, and they can be a few times to dozens of times heavier than the Sun. There are also supermassive black holes, which are millions or even billions of times heavier than the Sun. Almost every large galaxy seems to have one at its centre. Our own galaxy, the Milky Way, has one called Sagittarius A star.

## How Do We Find Something Invisible?

If black holes are black, how do scientists know they are there? One way is to watch stars. Astronomers saw stars near the centre of the Milky Way moving in fast, tight orbits around something heavy that they could not see. Only a black hole could explain it.

Another clue comes from gas. When gas falls towards a black hole, it spins around it in a flat disc, rubs against itself and becomes extremely hot. This hot gas glows brightly before it disappears. In 2019, a team using telescopes around the world, called the Event Horizon Telescope, released the first real image of a black hole in the galaxy M87. It showed a dark shadow surrounded by a glowing ring. In 2022 they released an image of the black hole at the centre of our own galaxy.

Scientists can even feel black holes. In 2015, detectors called LIGO picked up tiny ripples in space and time, known as gravitational waves. They came from two black holes crashing into each other more than a billion years ago. This discovery won a Nobel Prize.

## Common Myths

Black holes do not wander around swallowing everything like cosmic vacuum cleaners. If our Sun were somehow replaced by a black hole of the same mass, Earth would keep orbiting just as it does now, although it would become very cold and dark. You only get pulled in if you come very close.

## Conclusion

Black holes are places where gravity wins completely. They form from dying giant stars, they sit at the hearts of galaxies, and they bend space and time around them. Studying them helps scientists test their best ideas about how the universe works, and there are still many mysteries waiting to be solved.
