On the evening of 23 August 2023, millions of people across India watched their screens in silence. A small lander called Vikram was slowly descending towards the surface of the Moon. When it touched down safely, the country erupted in celebration. With Chandrayaan-3, India became the first country ever to land near the Moon's south pole, and only the fourth country to make a soft landing on the Moon at all.

## The Story Begins: Chandrayaan-1

The word Chandrayaan means moon vehicle in Sanskrit. India's space agency, ISRO, launched its first Moon mission, Chandrayaan-1, in 2008. It did not land, but it orbited the Moon and studied its surface. One of its instruments, together with data from NASA, helped confirm the presence of water molecules on the Moon. This was an exciting discovery, because water could one day help astronauts live and work there.

## Learning from Failure: Chandrayaan-2

In 2019, ISRO launched Chandrayaan-2. It had an orbiter, a lander called Vikram and a small rover called Pragyan. The orbiter worked well and is still studying the Moon. But during the final moments of the landing, the lander lost control and crashed.

It was a painful moment, but the scientists did not give up. They studied exactly what had gone wrong. For the next mission, they made the lander's legs stronger, gave it more fuel, added extra sensors and allowed it to land safely in a much larger area. They tested it again and again under many different conditions. The lesson was clear: failure is part of science, and it can be the first step towards success.

## Chandrayaan-3: Landing at the South Pole

Chandrayaan-3 was launched on 14 July 2023 from Sriharikota in Andhra Pradesh. Instead of using a giant, expensive rocket to fly straight to the Moon, it took a slower route, circling Earth several times to gain speed before heading to the Moon. This saved fuel and money. The whole mission cost far less than many space films made in Hollywood.

The landing site near the south pole was chosen for a reason. Some craters there are always in shadow and may hold frozen water. After landing, the Pragyan rover rolled out and explored the surface for about two weeks, which is one lunar day. It confirmed the presence of sulphur in the soil and measured other elements. Instruments on the lander measured the temperature of the soil and even recorded a natural movement of the ground, a possible moonquake. Near the end of the mission, the team fired Vikram's engines to make it hop about forty centimetres and land again. This small test showed that a future lander might be able to lift off from the Moon and bring samples back to Earth.

## Why It Matters

The success of Chandrayaan-3 showed that India can carry out complex space missions with careful planning and a small budget. It inspired many young people to take an interest in science and engineering. The Indian government declared 23 August as National Space Day to remember the landing, and the landing spot was named Shiv Shakti Point.

The mission also matters for the world. Many countries are planning to return to the Moon, and the south pole is a key target because of its ice. The information from Chandrayaan-3 adds to global knowledge. India has since continued with other missions, such as Aditya-L1, which studies the Sun, and it is working towards sending its own astronauts into space.

## Conclusion

Chandrayaan-3 is a story of ambition, patience and learning from mistakes. After the disappointment of 2019, ISRO came back stronger and achieved a world first. For students everywhere, it is a reminder that big dreams are possible when people keep trying and work together.
