A thin boy with untidy black hair, round glasses and a lightning-shaped scar on his forehead: millions of readers can recognise him from that description alone. He is Harry Potter, the hero of one of the most successful book series ever written. His adventures at a school for witches and wizards turned a generation of children into keen readers and made his story a worldwide cultural event.

## How the Story Began

The author, J.K. Rowling, has said that the idea for Harry came to her in 1990 while she was sitting on a delayed train from Manchester to London. Over the next few years, she planned the whole series and wrote the first book, often in cafés, while facing difficult times as a single mother with very little money. Several publishers rejected the book before a small publisher in London agreed to print it.

Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone was published in 1997. In the United States it was called Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone. Word spread quickly among children, parents and teachers. Six more books followed, the last one in 2007. By then, new books were released at midnight to huge crowds of excited fans, many dressed in robes and wizard hats.

## Who Is Harry Potter?

At the start of the story, Harry is an orphan living with his unkind aunt, uncle and cousin, the Dursleys, who make him sleep in a cupboard under the stairs. On his eleventh birthday, a giant named Hagrid arrives with surprising news: Harry is a wizard, and he has a place at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry.

Harry also learns the truth about his past. When he was a baby, a powerful dark wizard called Lord Voldemort killed his parents and tried to kill him too. But the curse failed and left only the scar. Voldemort lost his power, and Harry became famous in the wizarding world as the boy who lived. Across the seven books, Voldemort slowly returns, and Harry must face him.

## Friends and Hogwarts

At Hogwarts, Harry is sorted into Gryffindor house, known for bravery. He soon makes two best friends. Ron Weasley comes from a large, warm, but poor wizarding family and is funny and loyal. Hermione Granger is brilliant, hard-working and often the one who solves problems with knowledge. Together, the three face dangers, argue, make up and support each other.

The magical world is full of memorable details: owls that deliver letters, moving staircases, talking portraits and a sport called Quidditch played on flying broomsticks. The wise headmaster, Albus Dumbledore, guides Harry, while the strict and mysterious teacher Severus Snape keeps readers guessing until the very end.

## Why Readers Love It

The series grows up with its readers. The first books are full of wonder and adventure, while the later ones become darker and more serious, dealing with death, grief and war. Readers watch Harry, Ron and Hermione change from children into young adults.

The books also explore important themes. They show that friendship and love can be stronger than power. Dumbledore tells Harry that our choices, far more than our abilities, show who we truly are. The story also challenges prejudice, as some wizards look down on people born to non-magical families.

## A Global Phenomenon

The Harry Potter books have sold hundreds of millions of copies and have been translated into more than eighty languages. They were made into eight hugely popular films, and inspired theme parks, a stage play and countless fan stories. Many adults today say that Harry Potter was the series that made them fall in love with reading.

## Conclusion

Harry Potter is a story about an ordinary-seeming boy who discovers an extraordinary world. Through courage, loyalty and the help of his friends, he stands up to evil. That simple but powerful message is why the boy who lived continues to live in the imaginations of readers everywhere.
