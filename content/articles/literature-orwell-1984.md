Big Brother is watching you. These four words are among the most famous in modern literature. They come from Nineteen Eighty-Four, a novel by the English writer George Orwell. Published in 1949, the book describes a frightening future in which the government watches every citizen, controls all information and punishes anyone who thinks differently. More than seventy years later, people still use its ideas to talk about power, privacy and truth.

## About the Author

George Orwell was the pen name of Eric Arthur Blair. He was born in 1903 in Motihari, in what is now Bihar in India, where his father worked for the British government. He later worked as a police officer in Burma, now Myanmar, and became uncomfortable with the unfairness of colonial rule. He fought in the Spanish Civil War and saw how propaganda and lies were used by different political groups.

These experiences made him strongly opposed to dictatorships of every kind. Before Nineteen Eighty-Four, he wrote Animal Farm, a short story in which farm animals overthrow their human owner, only to be ruled by cruel pigs. Orwell was very ill with tuberculosis while writing Nineteen Eighty-Four, and he died in 1950, just a few months after it was published.

## The Story

The novel is set in Oceania, one of three huge states that are always at war. Oceania is ruled by the Party, whose leader is a mysterious figure called Big Brother. His face appears on posters everywhere. In every home there is a telescreen, a device that shows propaganda but also watches and listens to the people. The Thought Police arrest anyone suspected of disloyal thoughts.

The main character, Winston Smith, works at the Ministry of Truth. Despite its name, his job is to change old newspaper articles so that history always agrees with what the Party says today. If the Party changes its mind, the past is changed too, and the old version disappears. Winston secretly hates the Party. He begins to keep a diary, which is a crime, and he falls in love with a young woman named Julia. Together they hope to join a secret resistance. The ending, which takes place in the terrifying Room 101, is one of the darkest in literature.

## Powerful Ideas

Orwell invented several ideas that people still use. Newspeak is the Party's new language, designed to have fewer and fewer words. The goal is to make rebellious thoughts impossible, because there will be no words to express them. Doublethink means believing two opposite ideas at the same time, such as the Party slogan War is Peace. A thoughtcrime is the crime of simply thinking something the Party does not allow.

The Party's most chilling aim is to control reality itself. If everyone is forced to agree that two plus two equals five, then truth no longer depends on facts, only on power.

## Why It Still Matters

The word Orwellian is now used to describe situations where governments or organisations use surveillance, propaganda or misleading language to control people. The phrase Big Brother is used for any form of constant monitoring. A popular reality television show even took its name from it.

In today's world of security cameras, smartphones, data collection and online misinformation, many readers feel the novel is more relevant than ever. It reminds us to question what we are told, to value free speech and to protect our privacy.

## Conclusion

Nineteen Eighty-Four is not just a story about a dark future. It is a warning about what can happen when power has no limits and truth becomes whatever the powerful say it is. By imagining the worst, Orwell encouraged readers to defend honesty, freedom and independent thinking.
